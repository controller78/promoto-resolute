FAQ

We already have three agencies on contingency. Why brief you as well?

Contingent is residual here. Retained and volume clients get the time. Another CV-spray desk on the same list will not change the longlist — it will add more of it. A proper brief, and a partner who understands the programme, will. If we cannot prioritise a contingent search, we will say so rather than pretend otherwise.

Do we have to retain you? We only wanted a confidential conversation.

The form is a conversation, not a contract. We read the brief, come back the next working day, and recommend retained, volume, or contingent based on what you actually need. If we cannot prioritise a contingent search, we will tell you that on the call. Nothing is signed because you sent a brief.

How quickly do you come back, and what happens after we send the brief?

Next working day. We read what you sent, call or email, and agree the model before any search starts. Confidential notes stay confidential — unadvertised replacements, board-level hires, commercially sensitive programmes. If anything in the brief is sensitive, put it in the notes field or say so on the call.

We have an internal TA team. Is this still relevant?

Yes. We complement internal recruitment and operational teams rather than competing with them. Secondment, process review, and building an internal function exist specifically for this buyer — a principal consultant inside your team, a documented function you can keep, or a review of where cost, quality and time are leaking. One senior vacancy can still run as retained or volume alongside that.

What does it cost — and is this a sales ambush?

There is no published fee card. That is normal for retained search. Commercial terms are explained on the call: retained (part up front, rest on success), volume (a set package of works), contingent (fee on success, when capacity allows), secondment (set costings, three-month minimum, no extra placement fees). The form is not an invoice. No obligation.
